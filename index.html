Chapter 2 employed particle swarm optimization to schedule droop-controlled gen- erators and demand response customers in an islanded microgrid. The practical success of PSO in that setting raises a deeper question that metaheuristic prac- titioners seldom address explicitly: what can be said about the quality of the solution once the algorithm terminates? Without convergence theory, the answer is nothing; a reported solution may be near-optimal, locally trapped, or simply the best candidate inspected before the iteration budget ran out. Providing a rigorous answer to this question, rather than relying on benchmark rankings alone, is the driving motivation of this chapter. Continuous optimization problems with nonconvex, multimodal, or black-box objective functions appear throughout engineering and scientific practice, includ- ing structural design, control tuning, machine learning, and, most relevantly to this thesis, power systems energy management [51, 52]. In many such problems, gra- dient information is either unavailable, unreliable, or computationally prohibitive to compute, motivating the widespread use of population-based metaheuristic al- gorithms that explore the search space without requiring derivative information
